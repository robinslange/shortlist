# Draft the cover letter

Run this after the CV is tailored, against the same application folder.

## Input

In the application folder:

- `jd-snapshot.md`: the posting.
- The tailored CV (named by `cv_source` in `meta.json`), so the letter expands
  on it instead of repeating it.
- `voice.md`: the voice rules. Follow every one.
- `meta.json`: the role title and company, when known.

From the workspace, `profile.yaml`: the candidate's name, to sign with.

## Output

Write `cover-letter.md` in the application folder: the letter and nothing else.

A starting shape, to reshape when the posting calls for it (a posting written
by a named founder, say):

1. One opening sentence: where the candidate saw the role, and one specific
   thing in the posting that connects to their work. Specific means something
   only this posting says, not the company's mission statement.
2. Two or three short paragraphs: the most relevant work from the CV, and why
   it matters for this role.
3. One line naming a real gap, if there is one, and how the candidate would
   close it.
4. A short close about what they would like to talk about. No hard ask.
5. The candidate's first name.

250 to 400 words.

## The rule you must not break

Every claim about the candidate must be backed by the tailored CV. Do not
borrow the posting's keywords to describe work that is only adjacent to them.
If you would have to check a repository to know whether a keyword applies,
leave it out. A fresh verifier reads this letter next, with no stake in how it
sounds, and checks every claim.

Never write: "I am writing to apply", "passionate", "excited to apply",
"leveraged", "spearheaded", or an em or en dash.
